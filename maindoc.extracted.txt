1. Executive Summary
RailMind AI is an agentic, AI-powered railway safety and security platform that converts existing passive
CCTV infrastructure into an intelligent behavioural monitoring network. Using a multi-stage
computer-vision pipeline combined with a three-tier agentic reasoning architecture, the system detects
high-risk passenger behaviours in real time, calculates dynamic risk scores, and dispatches automated
alerts to railway staff — all without installing any new hardware or performing facial recognition.
The platform's core classifier is a bidirectional LSTM network trained on 30-second behavioural
sequences, enabling it to capture temporal dependencies that static classifiers miss. This temporal
awareness is central to distinguishing genuine distress from ordinary passenger behaviour, significantly
reducing false positives.
The platform addresses two critical challenges: suicide prevention and in-station crime (primarily
pickpocketing). Designed for rapid deployment via a cloud-based SaaS model, RailMind AI is both
scalable and cost-effective, requiring no additional hardware investment.

2. Problem Statement
Railway stations are high-density public spaces where critical incidents — suicide attempts, pickpocketing,
harassment, and unauthorised track access — can occur with little warning. Despite extensive CCTV
coverage, most surveillance systems are passive and rely entirely on human operators to monitor multiple
video feeds simultaneously. This makes it extremely difficult to identify subtle behavioural warning signs
and respond before an incident occurs.
Core Limitations of Existing Systems
(cid:127) Human attention fatigue — operators monitoring dozens of feeds simultaneously miss critical cues.
(cid:127) Reactive posture — current systems record incidents rather than prevent them.
(cid:127) Subtle signals overlooked — prolonged edge proximity, repetitive pacing, and social withdrawal go
undetected.
(cid:127) Delayed response — by the time an operator notices and dispatches staff, intervention windows are
missed.
(cid:127) Scalability gap — adding more cameras increases the monitoring burden without improving
outcomes.
Why This Problem Matters
Impact Area Consequence
Human Lives Preventable suicide attempts and assaults occur due to delayed or absent response.
Passenger Safety Pickpocketing and harassment create unsafe, hostile station environments.
Service Disruption Incidents cause platform closures, train delays, and cascading network disruptions.
Financial Loss Emergency responses, legal liability, and disruptions cost railway operators millions annually.
Staff Wellbeing Operators suffer psychological burden from witnessing preventable tragedies in real time.
Public Trust Repeated safety failures erode passenger confidence in public rail infrastructure.
The Opportunity
Many incidents could be prevented if suspicious or high-risk behaviours were detected early enough for
timely intervention. There is a clear and growing need for an intelligent, scalable, and privacy-preserving
solution that can proactively analyse human behaviour, identify potential risks in real time, and assist
railway staff before an incident occurs — not after.

3. Project Overview
RailMind AI is an agentic AI-powered railway safety and security system that transforms existing CCTV
cameras into intelligent behavioural monitoring agents. The system analyses human movement patterns
and detects abnormal or high-risk behaviours in real time using a layered pipeline of computer vision, pose

5. System Architecture
RailMind AI follows a layered pipeline architecture where each stage processes the output of the previous
stage, progressively transforming raw video into actionable intelligence. The architecture is split into an
Edge Tier (low-latency CV processing) and a Cloud Tier (reasoning, storage, and dashboard).
Stage Component Tier Description
Input CCTV Camera Feed Edge Existing cameras stream H.264/H.265 via RTSP/ONVIF.
Stage 1 OpenCV Processing Edge Decode stream, normalise frames, apply preprocessing filters.
Stage 2 YOLOv8 Detection Edge Detect all persons in frame with bounding boxes + confidence.
Stage 3 ByteTrack Edge Assign persistent IDs, track individuals across frames.
Stage 4 YOLOv8-Pose Edge Extract 17 body keypoints per person per frame.
Stage 5 Feature Extraction Edge Compute 7 behavioural features per tracked individual.
Stage 6 LSTM Classifier Edge Classify 30-second behavioural sequences into risk categories.
Stage 7 Reasoning Agent Cloud LangGraph agent evaluates context, produces final risk score.
Stage 8 Intervention Agent Cloud Determines and executes appropriate response actions.
Stage 9 PostgreSQL/SQLite Cloud Store incident records, alerts, analytics data.
Stage 10 React Dashboard Cloud Live alerts, incident history, heatmaps, analytics.

YOLOv8n/s detects all persons present in each frame with high speed and accuracy, producing bounding

(cid:127) Model: YOLOv8n (speed) or YOLOv8s (accuracy)
(cid:127) Confidence threshold: 0.45 (configurable)
(cid:127) Inference time: ~12ms per frame on GPU
(cid:127) Output: Bounding boxes [x1, y1, x2, y2] + confidence score
Step 3: Multi-Object Tracking
ByteTrack assigns a persistent numeric ID to each detected person and maintains track continuity

(cid:127) Algorithm: ByteTrack — byte-level detection association
(cid:127) Occlusion handling: Kalman filter prediction during missed detections
(cid:127) Max track age: 30 frames (3 seconds at 10 FPS)
(cid:127) Output: Track ID + bounding box per frame

YOLOv8-Pose extracts 17 COCO body keypoints per tracked individual — enabling analysis of posture,
gestures, lean angle, and body orientation.
(cid:127) Keypoints: Nose, eyes, ears, shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles
(cid:127) Output: 17 x [x, y, confidence] per person
(cid:127) Applications: Head tilt, arm position, body lean, crouching detection, edge-facing orientation

Step 5: Behavioural Feature Extraction
The Feature Extraction module aggregates raw tracking and pose data across a 30-second sliding
window to compute a 7-dimensional behavioural feature vector per tracked individual.
(cid:127) edge_proximity: Cumulative seconds within 0.5m of platform edge
(cid:127) loitering_time: Stationary duration in a single spatial zone
(cid:127) pacing_count: Number of back-and-forth movement cycles detected
(cid:127) movement_speed: Average velocity (m/s) over the window
(cid:127) direction_changes: Heading reversals per minute
(cid:127) following_distance: Sustained proximity (metres) to a single other person
(cid:127) crowd_interactions: Count of unique close-contact individuals
Step 6: LSTM Temporal Classification
A bidirectional LSTM network analyses the sequence of feature vectors over the 30-second window to
classify behaviour into one of four risk categories. Unlike static classifiers, the LSTM models how
behaviour evolves over time.
(cid:127) Input: Sequence of 30 feature vectors (one per second) — shape [30, 7]
(cid:127) Architecture: 2-layer Bidirectional LSTM + Dense + Softmax
(cid:127) Output classes: Normal, Suicide Risk, Pickpocketing Risk, Security Threat
(cid:127) Output: Class label + confidence score (0.0 - 1.0)
(cid:127) Inference time: < 5ms on GPU
Step 7: Reasoning Agent
A LangGraph-based Reasoning Agent receives the LSTM classification and evaluates multi-factor
context — beyond raw classification — to produce a nuanced final risk score.
(cid:127) Inputs: LSTM label, confidence score, location zone, behaviour duration, crowd density, historical
incident data
(cid:127) Processing: LangGraph graph workflow with conditional branching
(cid:127) Output: Risk Score (0-100) + Category + Recommended Action + Reasoning Summary
(cid:127) Example output: Risk Score 88, Category: Suicide Risk, Action: Approach immediately and offer
assistance

8. LSTM Behaviour Classifier — Deep Dive
The LSTM classifier is the analytical heart of RailMind AI. Unlike static classifiers that evaluate a single
snapshot of features, the LSTM processes a 30-second temporal sequence, allowing it to model how
behaviour evolves over time — which is essential for detecting patterns like gradual edge approach,
progressive pacing escalation, and sustained following behaviour.
Why LSTM over Static Classifiers
Criterion Static Classifier (e.g. XGBoostB)idirectional LSTM (RailMind AI)
Input Single feature vector snapshot 30-second feature sequence [30 x 7]
Temporal awareness None — no time context Full — models sequence evolution
Behavioural trajectory Cannot model progression Detects gradual escalation patterns
Ambiguous cases High false positive rate Context resolves ambiguity
Inference time < 1ms < 5ms — still real-time capable
Training complexity Low Moderate — requires sequence data
Production suitability Hackathon prototype Production-grade
Model Architecture
Input fi BiLSTM Layer 1 (128 units) fi Dropout (0.3) fi BiLSTM Layer 2 (64 units) fi
Dropout (0.3) fi Dense (32, ReLU) fi Dense (4, Softmax)
(cid:127) Input shape: [batch, 30, 7] — 30 timesteps, 7 features
(cid:127) Bidirectional: processes sequence forward and backward, capturing both historical build-up and
future context within the window
(cid:127) Layer 1 — 128 units: captures low-level temporal feature patterns
(cid:127) Layer 2 — 64 units: captures higher-level behavioural abstractions
(cid:127) Dropout 0.3: regularisation to prevent overfitting on limited training data
(cid:127) Output: softmax probabilities over 4 classes [Normal, Suicide Risk, Pickpocketing, Security Threat]
Training Strategy
(cid:127) Loss function: Categorical cross-entropy with class weighting (Suicide Risk class weighted 3x due to
imbalance)
(cid:127) Optimiser: Adam (lr=0.001) with ReduceLROnPlateau scheduler
(cid:127) Data augmentation: Gaussian noise injection on feature sequences to improve generalisation
(cid:127) Validation: Stratified 80/10/10 train/validation/test split
(cid:127) Hackathon phase: Trained on synthetic data generated from behavioural rule definitions
(cid:127) Production phase: Retrain on real incident-labelled data collected from deployed stations
(cid:127) Target accuracy: >92% on held-out test set

9. Agentic AI Framework
RailMind AI uses a three-tier agentic architecture built with LangGraph. Each agent has a defined role,
strict input/output contract, and operates as a node in the LangGraph state graph — enabling conditional
branching, parallel processing, and audit-friendly reasoning traces.
Perception Receives raw detection and tracking data. Runs pose estimation and feature extraction.
Agent Assembles the 30-second temporal feature sequence for the LSTM classifier.
(cid:127) Input: Video frames, YOLOv8 bounding boxes, ByteTrack IDs
(cid:127) Processing: Pose extraction fi per-frame feature computation fi 30s sequence
assembly fi LSTM inference
(cid:127) Output: {track_id, lstm_label, confidence, feature_sequence, timestamp,
location_zone}
Reasoning Receives the LSTM classification output. Evaluates multi-factor context — behaviour
Agent type, confidence, duration, location zone, crowd density, historical patterns — to
produce a final risk score with full reasoning trace.
(cid:127) Input: LSTM label, confidence, feature_sequence summary, location, historical
incident data
(cid:127) Processing: LangGraph conditional graph — context evaluation fi score computation
fi action determination
(cid:127) Output: {risk_score: 0-100, category, confidence, recommended_action,
reasoning_summary}
Intervention Receives the risk score and recommended action. Executes the appropriate response,
Agent creates the incident record, starts the acknowledgement timer, and handles escalation.
(cid:127) Input: Risk score, category, location, recommended action, staff availability map
(cid:127) Processing: Threshold evaluation fi action selection fi WebSocket dispatch fi
incident creation fi escalation timer
(cid:127) Output: Alert dispatched, incident record written, escalation timer active

15. Privacy & Ethics Framework
Privacy and ethics are foundational design constraints — every architectural decision was evaluated
against them. RailMind AI is built to be deployable in any jurisdiction without requiring special biometric
data processing exemptions.
No Facial Recognition
Face detection models are explicitly excluded. All analysis uses body movement, posture, and spatial
behaviour only. The system cannot identify who a person is.
No Biometric Storage
Track IDs are numeric, session-scoped integers not linked to any identity database. A track ID expires
when the session ends and cannot be correlated across sessions.
Minimal Data Retention
Raw video is retained for 72 hours by default. Only flagged incident clips are archived, configurable up to
30 days. Routine footage is not stored.
Behaviour-Only Analysis
The LSTM feature vector contains only anonymised movement metrics. No appearance, demographic, or
identity information is processed at any stage.
Human-in-the-Loop
All alerts require human staff confirmation before physical intervention. The AI recommends — humans
decide. No automated physical interventions.
Operator Accountability
Every alert, dispatch, acknowledgement, and resolution is immutably logged with a timestamp and
operator ID.
Transparent Reasoning
Every incident record includes the full LSTM confidence, risk score computation, and LangGraph
reasoning trace for explainability and audit.
Regulatory Alignment
Architecture is designed for GDPR (EU), PDPA (India), and equivalent data protection frameworks. Legal
review required per deployment region.
Bias Monitoring
Regular audits of alert rates by platform, time, and incident type to detect and correct potential algorithmic
bias in the LSTM classifier.
Public Disclosure
RailMind AI deployment is recommended to be disclosed via station signage, consistent with standard
CCTV notification requirements.

17. False Positive Handling
Alert fatigue is the leading cause of operator disengagement from AI safety systems. RailMind AI
implements a five-layer strategy to keep false positive rates below 8%, preserving operator trust while
maintaining high sensitivity for genuine risks.
Layer 1 — LSTM Confidence Thresholding
Alerts are only generated when LSTM confidence exceeds 0.75. Low-confidence predictions are silently
logged for retraining data collection but never sent to operators.
Layer 2 — Temporal Confirmation Window
High-risk classification must be sustained for 15+ continuous seconds before triggering an alert. This
eliminates single-frame anomalies and transient edge contacts caused by alighting passengers.
Layer 3 — Contextual Suppression
The system learns peak-density time windows (train arrivals, rush hour) from historical data and
automatically raises alert thresholds during these periods to suppress expected edge-proximity spikes.
Layer 4 — Operator Feedback Loop
Staff can mark any alert as a False Positive via the dashboard in one click. Labelled false positives are
accumulated for weekly LSTM retraining cycles, progressively improving model accuracy.
Layer 5 — Station Performance Monitoring
Automated system alerts flag any station where false positive rate exceeds 40% over a 7-day rolling
window. This triggers model recalibration and a review of local edge zone configuration.

18. Improvements & Recommendations
The following improvements are recommended for the production deployment path beyond the hackathon
prototype.
Multi-Camera Person Re-Identification
The current system tracks individuals within a single camera's field of view. Production deployment
requires re-identifying the same person across multiple camera feeds on a platform.
(cid:127) Approach: Appearance-based re-ID using lightweight OSNet or FastReID at the cloud tier
(cid:127) Benefit: Continuity of behavioural tracking across camera handoffs
(cid:127) Privacy note: Re-ID features are session-scoped and never stored as identity signatures
LSTM Continual Learning Pipeline
The LSTM classifier should improve continuously as labelled incident data accumulates from deployed
stations.
(cid:127) Weekly retraining: Incorporate operator-labelled false positives and confirmed incidents
(cid:127) A/B shadow testing: New model versions run in parallel with production before promotion
(cid:127) Versioning: All model versions retained with performance metrics for rollback capability
(cid:127) Target: 2-3% accuracy improvement per quarter in early deployment phase
Automated PA System Integration

20. Conclusion
RailMind AI represents a meaningful, technically grounded step forward in public safety technology —
transforming the role of CCTV from passive recording to active, intelligent prevention. By combining
state-of-the-art computer vision, a temporally-aware LSTM behaviour classifier, multi-agent reasoning, and
a privacy-first design philosophy, the system addresses one of the most challenging problems in railway
operations: detecting and preventing incidents before they occur.
The LSTM-based temporal classifier is a deliberate architectural choice that distinguishes RailMind AI from
simpler rule-based or static ML approaches. Modelling behavioural sequences over time — rather than
isolated feature snapshots — is essential for reducing false positives and detecting the gradual escalation
patterns that precede most critical incidents.
The architecture is technically sound, the problem domain is genuine and underserved, and the
privacy-preserving approach removes a significant barrier to institutional adoption. The improvements
outlined — continual learning, multi-camera re-ID, edge deployment, and the ethical framework — provide
a clear and credible path from hackathon prototype to production-grade deployment.
With the right investment in data collection, model refinement, and regulatory engagement, RailMind AI
has the potential to become a standard component of railway safety infrastructure — making stations safer
for passengers, more manageable for operators, and more accountable for railway authorities worldwide.
